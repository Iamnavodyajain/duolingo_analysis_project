Executive summary

High-level story

Project date: December 3, 2025

This analysis uses users, sessions, lessons, retention and A/B test data to evaluate a Duolingo‑style product through the AARRR lens. Key problem: strong activation but steep early-week drop-off (D1 → D7). Experiment Variant B improved conversion and should be rolled out while monitoring retention and monetization signals.

Acquisition

Top countries: India, Philippines, Germany, France, Brazil, US, Mexico, Spain. Device spread: iOS / Web / Android nearly equal. Mobile-first product—prioritize app onboarding and OS-specific performance.

Activation

Activated users ≈ 9,000 (activation rate ≈ 90%). Avg lessons per user ≈ 6.1; Avg time spent ≈ 60.8 minutes. Strong first-touch value — optimize to convert the remaining ~10%.

Retention

Day1 = 60% • Day3 = 30% • Day7 = 20% • Day30 = 0% (synthetic-data artifact). Primary leakage occurs in first week, with 40% churn by Day 1 and 70% churn by Day 7.

A/B test — primary result

Control → 5.6% conversion • Variant B → 8.34% conversion • p-value < 0.0001. Variant B shows ~48.9% relative lift. Recommendation: rollout Variant B and monitor retention & engagement for adverse effects.

Stat test

t-test / proportion test used in notebook. p < 0.0001 suggests statistical significance.

Churn analysis — key drivers

Churn peaks early: D1 churn ≈ 40% (complements 60% D1 retention). Logistic model (balanced) produced ~51.9% accuracy in synthetic data. Top actionable signals: streak length, XP, country, device (iOS better), language. Use these for targeted interventions.

Top churn segments

Low streak users, users earning <10 XP in first session, users in Hindi/Chinese language segments in this dataset, certain age groups (younger) show higher early churn.

Recommended model actions

Add temporal features (recency/frequency), rolling XP, session intervals. Try tree-based models + SHAP for feature explainability.

Detailed AARRR Deep‑Dive

Acquisition — where users come from

Top countries: India, Philippines, Germany, France, Brazil, US, Mexico, Spain. Age groups concentrated under 34. Device split shows a mobile-first audience where iOS users retain slightly better on average.

Activation — how users reach moment of value

Activation metric used: completion of at least one lesson on Day 1. Activated users ≈ 9k (≈90% of signups). Behaviors correlated with activation: first‑session XP & lesson completion speed. Bottlenecks: 10% fail to complete first lesson — simplify UX and add immediate reward to reduce friction.

Retention — cohort & segment patterns

Day1 = 60%, Day3 = 30%, Day7 = 20%, Day30 = 0% (synthetic artifact). Retention strongest in Germany, Spain, Indonesia; web users show slightly better D1 retention. Primary drop: D1→D3 (30% loss). Opportunity: targeted Day3 nudges and micro-goals for first week.

Revenue proxies & high‑value users

High-value proxies: sustained streaks, top XP decile, high lessons/day. Use these to seed trial offers and lookalike audiences for paid acquisition.

Referral / Viral potential

Referral is not measured directly. Infer referrers as users with high Day30 retention and long streaks. Recommend social share cards + small referral incentives tied to streak milestones.

Recommendations — Practical & prioritized

Acquisition

Focus ads & localization in PH, MX, DE; create language-targeted creatives for Hindi/Spanish learners.

Activation

Simplify the first lesson (1–2 minutes), add immediate XP/gem reward and visual progress indicator; nudge users who sign up but don't complete first lesson within 20–30 minutes.

Retention

Implement Day3 & Day7 re-engagement flows with tailored content and micro-rewards (3-day streak badge); A/B test content timing.

Revenue opportunities

Target premium trials to users with streak ≥7 or top XP decile; test gated advanced lessons and seasonal promo bundles.

Experiments

Test personalized lesson recommendations vs generic; test subtle pricing experiments for trial conversion in high-value segments.

Churn reduction

Introduce streak-protection tokens, recovery nudges, and language-specific onboarding tweaks for churn-prone cohorts.

Insights — short, shareable bullets

  • Mobile-first audience; prioritize app onboarding and OS performance tuning.
  • Activation is strong (~90%) — focus on converting the remaining ~10% with micro‑UX fixes.
  • Primary leakage: 40% churn by Day 1, 70% churn by Day 7 — implement Day3/Day7 re-engagement sequences.
  • High XP & streak users are the best candidates for premium offers.
  • Variant B shows ~48.9% lift; rollout but monitor retention & monetization.
  • Localization (Hindi/Spanish/Chinese) can reduce churn for specific cohorts.
  • Use behavioral signals to seed lookalike audiences for paid acquisition.
  • Design experiments to test personalization; measure lift on D7 and D30, not just conversion.
  • Consider a "streak-first" campaign to drive habit formation in early week.
